# Security review of F5 to F8 (bundle.rs, grant.rs, escrow.rs, register_in_bundle)

Read as an attacker, at interface 0.0.7. Decisions of the coordinator (ADR-40) were then built as interface 0.0.8: F1, F2 and F5 are fixed, F6 to F10 are accepted risks, both described below. This is a list of what was tested and what was found; it is not a statement
that the code is safe. "Test" means a test that exists in the repository; "read" means I followed the code and wrote no test.

## Found and fixed (the test failed first, the fix changes no ABI)
| # | Finding | Test | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F3 | `on_escrow_resolved` read the result of `nft_transfer` with `promise_result_checked(0, 64)` and treated every `Err` as a failure. A NFT contract that returns more than 64 bytes makes the call a *success* that the circle reads as a *failure*: the payment is taken back while the NFT already moved to the buyer (the buyer keeps the NFT for free; the escrow says `held`). | `escrow::tests::a_nft_contract_that_returns_a_long_value_still_delivers` (failed before) | only `PromiseError::Failed` is a failure; `TooLong` is a success (CT-075) |
| F4 | `revoke_grant(.., defaulted)` was open to anybody when the grantee had a defaulted D5 position in the running epoch or the two before it, even after the member was reactivated. A stranger could revoke a grant that the member accepted after its reactivation. | `grant::tests::a_reactivated_member_keeps_its_grants_against_a_stranger` (failed before) | the position counts only while the member is not `active`; `suspended` still counts for anybody, as in spec 15.3 (CT-071) |

## Fixed in 0.0.8 (decision of the coordinator, ADR-40; the test failed first)
| # | Finding | Test | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | **High.** `nft_on_transfer` is a public method and trusted both its caller and `previous_owner_id`. Any account could call it directly with the grantor as previous owner and fill the escrow slot of somebody else's offered class A grant with a worthless token or a fake NFT contract. The real grantor then could not deposit (`ERR_ESCROW_EXISTS`), and a buyer who paid got the fake token delivered while the obligation to the grantor stayed. | `escrow::tests::an_account_that_is_not_the_nft_of_the_grant_cannot_fill_the_escrow` (was ignored and failed), `the_right_nft_contract_with_another_token_cannot_fill_the_escrow`, `the_named_nft_contract_and_token_fill_the_escrow`; `ct070_grants::a_class_a_offer_must_name_its_nft_and_token`; `ct075_escrow` | `offer_grant` for class A requires `{"nft_contract": ..., "token_id": ...}` in `limits.scope` (`ERR_BAD_GRANT` otherwise); `nft_on_transfer` refuses another predecessor or token with `ERR_ESCROW_NFT_MISMATCH`. |
| F2 | **High for liveness.** Storage that members cause is paid by the balance of the circle account, not by the caller. `post_intent` takes 102 bytes per call (measured in near-sandbox) and nothing was attached or removed; one active member could post without limit, and bundles between two members could use up the quota of others. | `bundle::security_tests::one_member_cannot_fill_the_storage_of_the_contract_with_intents` (was ignored and failed), `the_65th_open_intent_of_a_member_is_refused_with_err_intent_limit`, `remove_intent_frees_a_slot_and_only_the_owner_can_remove`; `ct065_bundle::a_participant_is_in_at_most_16_bundles_per_epoch`; `ct_security_review::posting_intents_grows_the_storage_of_the_contract_and_asks_nothing_of_the_caller` (102 bytes, unchanged) | Limits, not deposits: `#[payable]` is refused because spec 9.3 item 2 says the facilitator, solver and keeper keys cannot attach a deposit. `post_intent`: at most `MAX_OPEN_INTENTS_PER_MEMBER` = 64 open per member (`ERR_INTENT_LIMIT`); new `remove_intent(intent_hash)`, owner only (`ERR_NOT_INTENT_OWNER`, `ERR_UNKNOWN_INTENT`), frees the slot. `commit_bundle`: at most `MAX_BUNDLES_PER_MEMBER_PER_EPOCH` = 16 for each participant (`ERR_BUNDLE_LIMIT`), counted per participant so two accounts cannot use up the slots of a third. Open grants were already capped at 16 per grantor. |
| F5 | `grants_of` returned every grant of an account and the index vector only grew. A sybil pair could add grants to the entry of a target until the view ran out of gas. The broker read `grants_of(lessee, grantee)` when a lessee defaulted and its watcher stopped on the error, so one poisoned account could hold up later events. | `ct070_grants::grants_of_pages_and_drops_ended_grants`; broker: `watcher.test` TC-BK-003-04, `grants-api.test`, `revoker.test` | `grants_of(account_id, role, from_index, limit)` lists open grants (`offered`, `active`) only: `revoke_grant` and the close of an escrow take the id out of both indexes, `get_grant` still answers for an ended grant. The broker lists the active grants of a lessee from the read API (`GET /grants?grantee=&status=active`) and confirms each with `get_grant`; the watcher logs an event whose handler failed and skips it (the sweep of the revoker still closes expired and unpaid leases). |

**Cleaning (F2).** Written first without a way to clean: the counter of bundles per (epoch, participant), the bundle records and the reservations stayed in state. `prune_epoch(epoch, max_items)` (spec section 4, permissionless, `finalized` epochs only, `ERR_NOT_FINALIZED` otherwise) now removes them with the pairs, the edge index and the participant totals of that epoch, and keeps every D5 position, the epoch record and the clearing proposal, so an unclaimed `claim` still works (`prune::tests::positions_and_the_claim_inputs_survive_a_full_prune`, `ct085_prune`). Intent records are per member and stay until `remove_intent`. Bundles committed under 0.0.7 have no per-epoch index and are not found.

## Accepted risks (decision of the coordinator, ADR-40; no code change)
| # | Finding | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F6 | The cap `max_search_share_bps` binds only the leg that the bundle declares as `search_leg`. A bundle without `search_leg`, or with the seeker paid through several payers, is not limited. The signers consent to what they sign, so this is no theft. (`ct_security_review::a_bundle_signature_is_good_for_its_own_terms_only` shows that a declared leg is part of the signed terms.) | Accepted. The cap binds the declared leg, and the marketplace of the backend always fills it. |
| F7 | A leg voucher names no bundle. `register_in_bundle(bundle_id, voucher, ..)` lets whoever holds the voucher choose the bundle, if the same pair is a leg of two bundles of the epoch. The debt and the obligation of the pair are the same either way; what moves is which bundle's reservation is released. | Accepted, low: no funds move. |
| F8 | `post_intent` is first come: whoever posts the hash of an intent first owns the `get_intent` record, and the real owner then gets `ERR_INTENT_EXISTS`. Nothing on chain uses the owner of an intent. | Accepted, low: no funds move. |
| F9 | A grant offered without a grantee can be accepted by any active member (first come). A member can squat offered grants; the grantor can revoke. | Accepted, low: no funds move. |
| F10 | An escrow in `completing` (callback never ran, for instance out of gas) or `stuck` keeps the NFT in the circle with no way out, and a `completing` pair stays locked for that epoch. The callback has 30 Tgas of its own. Unit tests cover `stuck`; no test covers a callback that does not run. | Accepted. There is no rescue path, because ADR-20 forbids the owner to move assets. The README states the risk. |

## Tested and held
- A bundle signature is good for its own terms only: changed amount, an extra leg, a reversed pair, or a `search_leg` that the signers did not sign give `ERR_BUNDLE_SIGNATURES` (`ct_security_review`).
- A payee cannot be dragged into a bundle: every participant, payees included, must sign (`ct_security_review`).
- Two bundles cannot reserve more than the headroom of a payer, and a refused bundle reserves nothing (`ct_security_review`, `ct065_bundle`).
- A leg voucher cannot go to a pair that is not a leg, beyond the leg, or in another epoch; nothing is booked when it is refused (`ct_security_review`, `ct065_bundle`).
- Class A: only the grantor can fill its own escrow, and only with the NFT contract and token named in the scope of the grant (F1) (`ct075_escrow`, token of another owner is returned); one buyer at a time; wrong payee, price, self purchase and unknown grant are refused; a failed delivery takes the registration back exactly, and refuses to when the epoch, the pair or the debt moved (unit tests, mutation of the guard fails four tests); `cancel_escrow` only by the grantor after the time; `revoke_grant` and `accept_grant` cannot take a held asset out of the escrow.
- Grants: a stranger cannot revoke with the reasons `grantor`, `grantee`, `operator`, an early `expired` or an early `defaulted` (`ct071_revoke_grant`); after F4, nor with `defaulted` against a reactivated member.

## Read, not tested
- Signatures and recipients: the signed message holds the circle account and the epoch and the NEP-413 hash holds the circle as recipient, so a signature does not carry to another circle account or epoch. I did not deploy two circles to prove it.
- Reentrancy: NEAR callbacks do not re-enter a receipt. The state of an escrow and the lock of the pair are written before the promise is created, and the callback is `#[private]`. I found no order in which a second call between the transfer and the callback changes the outcome (`complete_escrow`, `cancel_escrow`, `revoke_grant` and `accept_grant` all refuse while the escrow is not `held`).
- `terms_message` writes only account ids, decimal numbers and fixed text, so no JSON injection.

## Measured later (near-sandbox, `tests/ct086_gas_limits.rs` and `tests/ct083_upgrade_from_0_0_7.rs`, 2026-11-03)
Gas is the gas burnt by a transaction in near-sandbox; the limit of a transaction is 300 Tgas. Views were called as transactions, because only a transaction reports its gas.

**`commit_bundle`.** Cycle through n members (n legs): n=2 5.1, 4 7.4, 8 11.9, 16 20.7, 32 38.7, 64 74.9 Tgas. Dense bundle (every ordered pair of k members is a leg): k=4 (12 legs) 7.9, k=8 (56) 15.7, k=12 (132) 26.1, k=16 (240) 39.5 Tgas. Gas is no limit: even 552 legs burnt 61.5 Tgas. The limit is the log: `bundle_committed` lists the legs (about 68 bytes each), and a log message is at most 16384 bytes, so 380 legs failed with a host error ("The length of a log message 25714 exceeds the limit 16384"), not with a contract error. Decision: `MAX_BUNDLE_LEGS` = 64, a bundle with more legs is refused with `ERR_BAD_BUNDLE` (existing code, no ABI change; unit test `a_bundle_with_more_than_64_legs_is_refused_with_err_bad_bundle`). The worst case under the cap is a 64-leg cycle through 64 members, 74.9 Tgas.

**`grants_of`.** Reading the index costs nothing noticeable (a page of 10 costs 3.3 to 3.4 Tgas whether the grantee holds 16 or 80 grants); each returned grant costs about 0.13 Tgas (80 grants in one page: 12.6 Tgas). A grantee list was measured to 80 grants, not beyond. A view has 200 Tgas, so one page of about 1400 grants is the most a caller can ask for before its own call fails; it hurts nobody else. Extrapolated from the figures above, not measured.

**`list_asset_classes`.** About 0.1 Tgas per returned entry: registry of 10, 50, 100, 200 entries with `limit` 1000 cost 3.1, 7.3, 12.5, 23.0 Tgas; `limit` 20 costs 3.1 to 4.4 Tgas at any size. Only the DAO adds entries.

**Upgrade from 0.0.7.** Tested with the real path (`propose_upgrade`, the timelock moved with `patch_state`, `apply_upgrade` with the new wasm, then `migrate`). Written before the upgrade under 0.0.7: a class B grant (accepted), a class A grant with its NFT in the escrow (scope `{}`), two intents, a bundle with reservations, a registered voucher. After the upgrade every view reads the same (`get_grant`, `get_escrow`, `get_bundle`, `get_intent`, `headroom`, `obligation`, `get_params`, `get_epoch`, `get_pool`, `list_asset_classes`). The paged `grants_of` lists the old index. An intent from before the upgrade has no counter: its owner can remove it and a stranger cannot. A new bundle works on top of the old reservations. A buyer completes the escrow that was filled under 0.0.7. A revoke after the upgrade takes the grant out of both indexes. Limits of the test: the root state of the contract did not change between 0.0.7 and 0.0.8, so `migrate` has nothing to convert; an upgrade from 0.0.4 or older (the `Params` layout changed in 0.0.5) was not tried. Bundles committed under 0.0.7 have no per-epoch index, so `prune_epoch` does not find them (their records stay).

## Economic attacks through bundles (`tests/ct084_bundle_economics.rs`, near-sandbox, 2026-11-03)
All three tests passed the first time they ran: I found no bug here, and I did not change the contract for this part. What was tried and what it showed:

1. **Wash trading (ADR-08).** Three bundles that are cycles through three members (10, 20 and 30 credit on every leg), every leg registered, everything cleared, `finalize_epoch`: no D5 position, `settled_history` stays 0 for all three, the limit of the next epoch is the base (80 of a stake of 100), debt 0 (`cycles_through_bundles_do_not_raise_a_limit`). Legs for cashback or a seeker share are ordinary legs, so they follow the same rule: only a residual that is paid in cash (`settle_transfer`) adds to `settled_history`. **What does work for an attacker, with or without a bundle:** two accounts of one actor leave a residual, one pays it in cash inside the payment window, the other claims it back. With the default fees (`clearing_fee_bps` 0) that round trip costs gas only. With the default `base_limit_ratio` 8000 and `limit_growth` 1000, the cash paid in one epoch is at most the limit, so the limit goes 80 -> 88 -> 96.8 -> 100 (the stake) in three epochs (computed from `compute_limit`; the growth is clamped by the stake, `TC-CT-038-03`). The loss is bounded by the stake, so ADR-18 and I-01 still hold; what the actor gains is 25% more credit on the same stake, free of charge. Whether that is acceptable is a decision for the spec: options are a non-zero fee on the residual that is paid, or counting only cash from counterparties the member has not paid back in the same epoch. Not built.
2. **A limit above the stake or above the headroom.** A payer with limit 80: a bundle reserves 60, a second bundle that needs 30 is refused `ERR_BUNDLE_HEADROOM`, plain vouchers can use the free 20 and the 21st credit is refused `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CREDIT`, a leg over its maximum is refused `ERR_BUNDLE_LEG_EXCEEDED`, and when the whole leg is registered the debt is exactly 80 (`debt_plus_reservation_never_passes_the_limit`). Registration through a leg does not read the limit, only the reservation made at commit; the reservation is made against `limit - debt - reserved`, and the limit can only fall at `finalize_epoch`, after the epoch of the bundle has ended. `limit <= stake` was checked on the member view.
3. **Clearing with a bundle that is registered in part, then `prune_epoch`.** One leg of a three-leg cycle registered for 4 of 10, close, clearing with no edge, residual paid, finalize: the epoch after has no reservation (`headroom == limit` for all), `prune_epoch` removes the bundle, its reservation and counters, the unclaimed position of the creditor is unchanged afterwards (`a_partly_registered_bundle_leaves_nothing_behind_after_prune`). Order of refusals seen: a voucher of a pruned epoch is refused by the epoch rules before the bundle is looked up.

Not tested in this part: a bundle whose payee is pruned from the circle by `exit`, and clearing with fees above 0 together with bundles.

## Not checked
- Economic attacks with bundles (wash trading through legs), interplay with clearing under many bundles.
- Fuzzing, and any attack through the SDK or the broker beyond `backend/docs/security-review-2.md`.
- Gas of `grants_of` for a grantee with more than 80 grants (extrapolated), and of `prune_epoch` at the 200-item cap.

## Escrow of a NEP-141 token (CT-090, CT-091), checked 2026-11-03
The class A escrow takes an exact amount of a fungible token as well as an NFT. The grantor names `{"ft_contract": ..., "amount": "..."}` in the scope, sends exactly that amount with `ft_transfer_call` and `msg` `{"action":"escrow","grant_id":N}`, and `complete_escrow` pays the buyer with `ft_transfer`. Every refusal at the receiver is a panic, so the token returns the whole amount to the sender; the tests check the balances, not only the error.

| Attack or failure | Result | Test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Another account puts the right amount against the grant of the grantor | `ERR_ESCROW_DEPOSITOR`, tokens back | `ct090_ft_escrow` |
| Amount below or above the scope | `ERR_ESCROW_FT_AMOUNT`, tokens back | unit + `ct090_ft_escrow` |
| A token contract other than the one in the scope | `ERR_ESCROW_FT_MISMATCH`, tokens back | unit + `ct090_ft_escrow` |
| A second deposit for the same grant | `ERR_ESCROW_EXISTS`, the first escrow is untouched | unit + `ct090_ft_escrow` |
| `stake` or `settle` sent through a token that is not the USDT | `ERR_WRONG_TOKEN`, no stake, no pool change | `ct090_ft_escrow` |
| The USDT of the circle as the escrow asset | `ERR_ESCROW_USDT` (it would mix with stakes and the pool) | unit + `ct090_ft_escrow` |
| Buyer not whitelisted, frozen, or not registered at the token | `ft_transfer` fails, the voucher is taken back exactly, escrow `held`, grant `offered`, no debt | `ct091_restricted_token` |
| Circle not whitelisted, or grantor frozen, at deposit time | the token refuses the transfer, no escrow, grantor keeps the tokens | `ct091_restricted_token` |
| Token that burns a fee on every transfer | the circle holds `amount - fee` and cannot send `amount`: a sale fails and is taken back, and so does `cancel_escrow`; the escrow stays `held`. The shortfall is the fee. | `ct091_restricted_token` |

A fee token cannot be refused at `ft_on_transfer`: the NEP-141 callback carries the amount the sender asked to move, not the amount the circle received, and finding out would take a balance call plus a per-token sum of held escrows (more state and a second callback). It is documented as unsupported: the sale and `cancel_escrow` fail without loss, the escrow stays `held`. After failed deliveries `cancel_escrow` still returns the tokens once the grant has expired (`the_grantor_gets_the_tokens_back_after_failed_deliveries`).

Limits that stay: a token that freezes the circle or the grantor after the deposit can keep the tokens in the circle for good, and `complete_escrow` / `cancel_escrow` then fail and are retried; a token with a transfer fee is a bad asset for this escrow, and the grantor should check the balance (`ft_balance_of` of the circle) before offering it. The scope says which contract and which amount, but not whether that token is worth anything: the buyer judges that, as with an NFT.
