# Security review of F5 to F8 (bundle.rs, grant.rs, escrow.rs, register_in_bundle)

Read as an attacker, at interface 0.0.7 (HEAD after the commits named below). This is a list of what was tested and what was found; it is not a statement
that the code is safe. "Test" means a test that exists in the repository; "read" means I followed the code and wrote no test.

## Found and fixed (the test failed first, the fix changes no ABI)
| # | Finding | Test | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F3 | `on_escrow_resolved` read the result of `nft_transfer` with `promise_result_checked(0, 64)` and treated every `Err` as a failure. A NFT contract that returns more than 64 bytes makes the call a *success* that the circle reads as a *failure*: the payment is taken back while the NFT already moved to the buyer (the buyer keeps the NFT for free; the escrow says `held`). | `escrow::tests::a_nft_contract_that_returns_a_long_value_still_delivers` (failed before) | only `PromiseError::Failed` is a failure; `TooLong` is a success (CT-075) |
| F4 | `revoke_grant(.., defaulted)` was open to anybody when the grantee had a defaulted D5 position in the running epoch or the two before it, even after the member was reactivated. A stranger could revoke a grant that the member accepted after its reactivation. | `grant::tests::a_reactivated_member_keeps_its_grants_against_a_stranger` (failed before) | the position counts only while the member is not `active`; `suspended` still counts for anybody, as in spec 15.3 (CT-071) |

## Found and not fixed (needs a decision, a new error code, a spec change or an ABI change)
| # | Finding | Evidence | Proposal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | **High.** `nft_on_transfer` is a public method and trusts both its caller and `previous_owner_id`. Any account can call it directly with the grantor as previous owner and fill the escrow slot of somebody else's offered class A grant with a worthless token or a fake NFT contract. The real grantor then cannot deposit (`ERR_ESCROW_EXISTS`), and a buyer who pays gets the fake token delivered while the obligation to the grantor stays. | `escrow::tests::an_account_that_is_not_the_nft_of_the_grant_cannot_fill_the_escrow`, ignored, fails today: `cargo test --lib -- --ignored an_account_that_is_not` | class A scope names the NFT contract (and the token): `offer_grant` for class A requires `{"nft_contract": ..., "token_id": ...}` in `limits.scope`, `nft_on_transfer` refuses another predecessor or token (`ERR_ESCROW_NFT_MISMATCH`), and `get_escrow` already shows what is held. Spec change for the class A scope; no new method. |
| F2 | **High for liveness.** Storage that members cause is paid by the balance of the circle account, not by the caller. `post_intent` takes 102 bytes of storage per call (measured in near-sandbox) and nothing is attached or removed later; one active member can post without limit. The same holds for the accepted-grant index of a grantee, the offered grants of many members, and bundles between two members. When the balance is below the storage staking, every call that grows state fails, `commit_epoch` and `finalize_epoch` among them. | `bundle::security_tests::one_member_cannot_fill_the_storage_of_the_contract_with_intents` (ignored, fails today: 5000 intents accepted); `ct_security_review::posting_intents_grows_the_storage_of_the_contract_and_asks_nothing_of_the_caller` (102 bytes) | `#[payable]` on `post_intent`, `offer_grant`, `accept_grant`, `commit_bundle` with a refundable storage deposit (ABI), or a cap per member and an expiry that lets the intent and the offered grant be deleted. The size of the cap is a product decision. |
| F5 | `grants_of` returns every grant of an account and the index vector only grows (a grantee's entry is added at each accept and never removed). A sybil pair can add grants to the entry of a target faster than anyone removes them, until the view runs out of gas. The broker reads `grants_of(lessee, grantee)` when a lessee defaults and its watcher stops on the error, so one poisoned account can hold up later events. | read; the view gas was not measured | `grants_of(account, role, from_index, limit)` (ABI), drop revoked ids from the index, and on the broker side list the active grants from the read API instead of the view and skip an event that fails after logging it. |
| F6 | The cap `max_search_share_bps` binds only the leg that the bundle declares as `search_leg`. A bundle without `search_leg`, or with the seeker paid through several payers, is not limited. The signers consent to what they sign, so this is no theft, but the parameter does not mean what its name says. | read; `ct_security_review::a_bundle_signature_is_good_for_its_own_terms_only` shows that a declared leg is part of the signed terms | document it as a limit on the declared leg, or add a rule on the total paid to one payee in a bundle (spec change). |
| F7 | A leg voucher names no bundle. `register_in_bundle(bundle_id, voucher, ..)` lets whoever holds the voucher choose the bundle, if the same pair is a leg of two bundles of the epoch. The debt and the obligation of the pair are the same either way; what moves is which bundle's reservation is released. | read | low; bind the bundle in `resource` of the voucher (SDK and spec) or forbid one pair in two bundles of an epoch. |
| F8 | `post_intent` is first come: whoever posts the hash of an intent first owns the `get_intent` record, and the real owner then gets `ERR_INTENT_EXISTS`. Nothing on chain uses the owner of an intent. | read | low; keep the intent book off chain only, or key the record by `(account, hash)`. |
| F9 | A grant offered without a grantee can be accepted by any active member (first come). A member can squat offered grants; the grantor can revoke. | read | low; allow `grantee` in `GrantInput` (ABI). |
| F10 | An escrow in `completing` (callback never ran, for instance out of gas) or `stuck` keeps the NFT in the circle with no way out, and a `completing` pair stays locked for that epoch. The callback has 30 Tgas of its own. | unit tests for `stuck`; no test for a callback that does not run | a rescue path would be a method that moves a held asset, which ADR-20 forbids for the owner; decide between that and accepting the loss. |

## Tested and held
- A bundle signature is good for its own terms only: changed amount, an extra leg, a reversed pair, or a `search_leg` that the signers did not sign give `ERR_BUNDLE_SIGNATURES` (`ct_security_review`).
- A payee cannot be dragged into a bundle: every participant, payees included, must sign (`ct_security_review`).
- Two bundles cannot reserve more than the headroom of a payer, and a refused bundle reserves nothing (`ct_security_review`, `ct065_bundle`).
- A leg voucher cannot go to a pair that is not a leg, beyond the leg, or in another epoch; nothing is booked when it is refused (`ct_security_review`, `ct065_bundle`).
- Class A: only the grantor can fill its own escrow with a real NFT (`ct075_escrow`, token of another owner is returned); one buyer at a time; wrong payee, price, self purchase and unknown grant are refused; a failed delivery takes the registration back exactly, and refuses to when the epoch, the pair or the debt moved (unit tests, mutation of the guard fails four tests); `cancel_escrow` only by the grantor after the time; `revoke_grant` and `accept_grant` cannot take a held asset out of the escrow.
- Grants: a stranger cannot revoke with the reasons `grantor`, `grantee`, `operator`, an early `expired` or an early `defaulted` (`ct071_revoke_grant`); after F4, nor with `defaulted` against a reactivated member.

## Read, not tested
- Signatures and recipients: the signed message holds the circle account and the epoch and the NEP-413 hash holds the circle as recipient, so a signature does not carry to another circle account or epoch. I did not deploy two circles to prove it.
- Reentrancy: NEAR callbacks do not re-enter a receipt. The state of an escrow and the lock of the pair are written before the promise is created, and the callback is `#[private]`. I found no order in which a second call between the transfer and the callback changes the outcome (`complete_escrow`, `cancel_escrow`, `revoke_grant` and `accept_grant` all refuse while the escrow is not `held`).
- `terms_message` writes only account ids, decimal numbers and fixed text, so no JSON injection.

## Not checked
- Gas of `commit_bundle` with many legs (it is quadratic in the participants; a failure there reverts the transaction).
- Gas of `grants_of` and `list_asset_classes` for large lists (F5).
- Economic attacks with bundles (wash trading through legs), interplay with clearing under many bundles, upgrade and `migrate` of the new storage keys.
- Fuzzing, and any attack through the facilitator, the broker or the SDK.
